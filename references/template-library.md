# Typographic poster template library

Choose a template by content shape and desired reading motion. These are generative systems, not layouts to trace. Change spans, line breaks, scale, family, and density for each brief.

## T1 — Monumental title split

**Best for:** short cultural titles, launches, festivals, covers.

- Make the title occupy 45–70% of the canvas in two or three unequal masses.
- Let one word, character, or numeral act as the hinge between masses.
- Place date as a compact second landmark; put venue and credits in one lower information strip.
- Pair heavy CJK display with lighter Latin or serif microcopy.
- Device: one reverse block or one controlled edge bleed.
- Avoid: breaking the title into unreadable fragments or letting metadata compete with it.

## T2 — Bilingual cascade

**Best for:** Chinese/English events, editorial covers, public programs.

- Stack Chinese and English in alternating scale, width, and weight to create a vertical reading cascade.
- Translate by semantic pair, not line-for-line mirroring. Keep paired meanings adjacent.
- Use 3–5 strong horizontal anchors with deliberately unequal line lengths.
- Pair a heavy or condensed CJK face with a neo-grotesk or extended Latin display face.
- Device: script interlock or one offset baseline.
- Avoid: identical bilingual duplication, random font switching, and equal-size paragraphs.

## T3 — Rotated metadata orbit

**Best for:** exhibitions with long titles, dates, venue, artists, and bilingual detail.

- Keep a stable stacked title column as the visual axis.
- Place opening, duration, venue, and address as complete tilted modules orbiting the title, usually within ±12°.
- Keep each module internally compact; labels and values rotate together.
- Use one neutral display sans family with different widths, plus a readable information face.
- Device: controlled rotation and diagonal counterflow.
- Avoid: rotating individual date pieces, scattering an address, or using more than three tilted modules.

## T4 — Sparse word field

**Best for:** conceptual exhibitions, premium announcements, artist posters.

- Let a very large mixed-script title traverse the central field while preserving 50–70% total open space.
- Isolate date in a tight corner module, artist/author in another small island, and venue near the footer.
- Vary width and weight across scripts but keep a consistent visual color.
- Device: one exaggerated line break, vertical word, or compact numeral stack.
- Avoid: filling the void with decorative microcopy; emptiness must frame the title and date.

## T5 — Editorial information matrix

**Best for:** profiles, brand facts, schedules, recruitment, information-heavy notices.

- Divide content into 3–6 semantic modules of unequal span and density.
- Use labels, rules, and local two-column structures inside modules.
- Give one title or number a dramatically larger scale so the result does not become a spreadsheet.
- Pair a display family with a restrained information family; use tabular numerals for facts.
- Device: one oversized numeral, one edge label, or one interrupted rule.
- Avoid: equal boxes, repeated card styling, and forced alignment that separates related content.

## T6 — Distributed manifesto

**Best for:** statements, studio communication, quotes, design commentary.

- Distribute 3–7 text islands across the canvas with one dominant phrase and one secondary response.
- Make density alternate: large statement, compact paragraph, isolated date or author.
- Use two contrasting type voices, such as heavy CJK display and monospaced/condensed Latin.
- Device: one icon row, vertical island, or footer counterweight.
- Avoid: filler text, equal loudness, and arbitrary scattering without a reading route.

## T7 — Edge spine and open core

**Best for:** lectures, forums, programs, serial events.

- Put series name, year, or peripheral metadata on a vertical or narrow edge spine.
- Use the open core for the main title and date; place speaker or venue in a lower anchored block.
- Align the spine and core through repeated baselines rather than identical widths.
- Device: vertical type or a repeated directional arrow used as navigation.
- Avoid: long body copy in vertical setting and decorative arrow chains with no meaning.

## T8 — Reverse-block anchor

**Best for:** short campaigns, publication covers, experimental but restrained announcements.

- Use one black rectangle containing a key word, title fragment, or date as the anchor.
- Balance it with a much larger black-on-white title or numeral elsewhere.
- Keep the remaining facts in one compact module rather than several mini boxes.
- Pair a bold display face with an editorial serif or neutral grotesk for contrast.
- Device: black/white reversal only; let the block supply the geometry.
- Avoid: multiple unrelated boxes, sticker-like labels, or color highlights competing with the anchor.

## Selection guide

- Very short title, high impact: T1 or T8.
- Bilingual title is itself the image: T2.
- Long exhibition title plus many facts: T3.
- Conceptual, premium, or artist-led with little copy: T4.
- Structured factual content: T5.
- Several statements or quotes: T6.
- Lecture, forum, or serial identity: T7.

Combine only one secondary behavior. Examples: T4 with T7 edge spine; T1 with T8 reverse anchor; T2 with a restrained T3 date module. Do not combine two complete templates or use every device at once.
