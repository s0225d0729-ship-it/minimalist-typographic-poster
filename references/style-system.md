# Monochrome typographic system

Use this reference to construct the chosen template. The target is a black-white-gray poster in which hierarchy, type contrast, spacing, and alignment create the image.

## Global grid, local grouping

- Set safe margins, 2–6 global columns, major horizontal zones, and a baseline unit before composing.
- Use the global grid for the outer frame, dominant title mass, major information modules, rules, and footer.
- Each semantic module may use its own local subgrid. Labels, values, translations, and date components should sit close enough to read as one unit.
- A module occupies a declared global span, but its internal lines do not need to touch every column edge.
- Semantic proximity outranks formal symmetry. Never scatter a date range, address, bilingual pair, or person/role across the canvas just to fill columns.
- Reconstructible alignment is required at the module level, not on every individual word.

Typical starting values: safe margin 5–8% of the short edge; 2–4 columns for sparse posters; 4–6 for dense or bilingual posters; baseline unit 4, 6, or 8 relative units. These are starting points, not a mandatory six-column recipe.

## Hierarchy engineering

Build contrast in this order:

1. scale discontinuity;
2. weight or width;
3. position and whitespace;
4. family or script contrast;
5. black/white reversal;
6. rule, border, arrow, underline, or gray.

Aim for one dominant mass, one secondary landmark, and a quieter information route. Avoid four equally loud zones. Use title strength 100, date 55–70, venue 30–45, and metadata 12–25 as a diagnostic ratio.

## Type-role pairing

Use up to three families and four roles:

- Display CJK: heavy/black, condensed, sharp sans, or restrained serif.
- Display Latin: neo-grotesk, geometric, condensed, extended, or serif counterpoint.
- Information: regular/medium readable face.
- Numerals: tabular or condensed figures, optionally shared with the Latin display family.

Recommended contrast patterns:

- heavy CJK grotesk + light/regular Latin grotesk + neutral information sans;
- condensed CJK display + extended Latin display + regular body;
- editorial CJK serif + neutral Latin sans + tabular numerals;
- one variable family using extreme width/weight contrast, plus one restrained counter-family.

Do not change typefaces line by line. Assign the role first, then keep that role consistent. Correct Chinese/Latin optical size, punctuation spacing, and baseline relationships manually.

## Reading motion and novelty

Select one primary motion: vertical cascade; left-to-right editorial sweep; diagonal orbit around a stable title; edge-to-center movement; or sparse field with isolated landmarks.

Select at most two devices: rotated module, edge spine, reverse block, interlocked scripts, monumental numeral, controlled bleed, asymmetric void, or directional rule. Rotation applies to a complete semantic module, normally within ±12°, not to disconnected words from the same fact.

## Whitespace and density

- Treat whitespace as a shape with a deliberate location.
- Sparse layouts may preserve 45–75% open field; dense layouts need at least one clear pause or void.
- Prefer one large quiet field over many incidental holes.
- Tight title leading is acceptable; information text needs readable leading.
- Dense information should form intentional texture without becoming a wall of equal-weight text.

## Marks, images, and color

- Rules, arrows, brackets, bullets, plus signs, circles, and borders are syntax, not decoration.
- Images are omitted by default. If necessary, keep a supplied portrait/product image small, rectangular, grayscale, and subordinate.
- Decorative shape count is zero by default; usually no more than two informational marks, one reverse block, and one border.
- Color is opt-in. Apply one requested color to one role, usually under 10% of the canvas.

## Audit

Revise if any two checks fail:

1. Does the title win at thumbnail size?
2. Is the date the next fastest fact to find?
3. Are venue/address and each person/role kept together?
4. Can the global grid be inferred from module edges and repeated anchors?
5. Do font changes signal roles rather than novelty for its own sake?
6. Is there one clear reading motion?
7. Is every required word fully readable at least once?
8. Would removing a mark or font reduce noise without harming meaning? If yes, remove it.

## Raster prompt scaffold

> 3:4 vertical monochrome typography-led poster, [template], hierarchy title → date → venue → metadata, exact text supplied separately, [N]-column global grid with semantic modules and local subgrids, [type-role pairing], [primary reading motion], [one or two novelty devices], crisp flat print design, active negative space, no cropped required text, no illustration, no decorative geometry, no gradient, no shadow, no mockup, no watermark, no extra text, no invented logo or QR code.

For text-heavy or accuracy-critical work, typeset natively in SVG, HTML/CSS, slides, or another editable layout format rather than relying on raster text generation.
